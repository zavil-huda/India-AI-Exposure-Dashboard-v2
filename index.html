Research Highlights

Key Findings

Before exploring the interactive visualizations, these headline findings summarize the broader patterns observed across India's occupational landscape. They provide context for interpreting the charts that follow and should be considered alongside the dashboard's methodology and limitations.

AI exposure is uneven across occupations

Generative AI is unlikely to influence every occupation equally. Knowledge-intensive and digitally enabled roles generally exhibit higher exposure than occupations centred on manual, physical, or location-based activities.

Exposure does not imply job replacement

Higher AI exposure should be interpreted primarily as an indicator of changing work tasks. In many occupations, AI is more likely to augment productivity, decision-making, and workflow efficiency than fully automate entire jobs.

Digital work shows the strongest transformation potential

Occupations involving documentation, communication, analysis, coding, research, or digital content creation generally demonstrate greater interaction with modern Generative AI capabilities than occupations requiring continuous physical presence.

Workforce size and AI exposure are independent

Large occupational groups are not necessarily the most AI-exposed. Compare both rectangle size and colour throughout the dashboard to understand how workforce scale and exposure differ across sectors.

How to interpret these findings

Treat these observations as starting points for exploration rather than final conclusions. Use the interactive treemap, comparison metrics, and supporting visualizations below to investigate why occupations differ, identify broader patterns, and evaluate how AI may reshape work across different parts of India's economy.

Scoring Framework

How AI Exposure Scores Are Interpreted

AI exposure scores estimate how compatible an occupation's core tasks are with current Generative AI capabilities. Higher scores indicate greater potential for AI-assisted work, not inevitable automation or job replacement.

0–2 minimal
2–4 low
4–6 moderate
6–8 high
8–10 very high

Prototype note: India does not yet publish a single official public dataset that gives occupation-level employment counts and AI exposure scores in the same ready-to-use structure as the US BLS dataset used by Karpathy’s visualizer. This build therefore combines official Indian macro sources and occupation standards with transparent synthesized occupation clusters suitable for a first-pass India replica.
